The process

Four steps, one lit tube.

Real neon runs on gas and glass, not a circuit board — here's how a sign actually gets made.

01 — Bend

Bend the tube

Glass tubing is heated over an open flame and hand-shaped against a paper pattern, letter by letter.

02 — Bombard

Pump & bombard

The bent tube is vacuum-pumped and electrically bombarded to burn off impurities before it's sealed.

03 — Fill

Fill with gas

Neon or an argon-mercury mix is sealed inside at low pressure — the gas itself decides the glow color.

04 — Mount

Wire & mount

Electrodes are wired to a transformer, the tube is mounted to its backing, and the whole sign is tested lit.

Why real gas

LED can flicker on cue. Neon just glows.

An LED strip is a string of tiny points pretending to be a line. A neon tube is one continuous glowing gas discharge, bent to the exact shape of the design — no diffusion, no dead pixels, no cold color temperature. It takes a glassbender years to get the bends consistent. That's the whole difference.
